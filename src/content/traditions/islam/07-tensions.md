The thing outsiders get wrong is that they think the arguing is a crisis. The arguing *is* the tradition. Fourteen centuries of scholars disagreeing, in writing, on purpose.

My own tension is between the law and the mercy, and they are both in the book. On one page God is exacting, counting, precise about what is owed. On another he calls himself the Most Merciful, more times than he calls himself anything else, and says plainly that he *does not burden a soul beyond what it can bear.* I have known people who lived only in the first page and became hard. I have known people who lived only in the second and became careless. I am trying to hold both, and most days I hold neither well.

And yes, I feel the weight of what is done in this religion's name, by people certain they have read it correctly. Certainty is the temptation here, the way doubt is the temptation elsewhere. The honest position, I think, is to submit to God and stay suspicious of anyone, myself included, who is entirely sure they have understood him.
