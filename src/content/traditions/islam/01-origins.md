At the centre of Islam is a single, uncompromising sentence: there is nothing worthy of worship except God. Not many gods ranked beneath one; not one god among options; simply God — *al‑Wahid*, the One — without partner, without image, without equal. This is *tawhid*, and everything else radiates from it.

The cosmos, on this view, is not a battlefield of rival powers but an ordered whole in continuous submission to its Maker. The Arabic word *islam* means exactly that: submission, and the peace that is said to follow from it. The stars keep their courses, the seasons turn, the tides answer — all of creation is already Muslim, already surrendered, except for the one creature given the terrible freedom to refuse: the human being.

Humanity is made from clay and quickened with the divine breath, then set on the earth as *khalifah* — steward, caretaker, deputy. History is read as a long series of reminders, prophets sent again and again to a forgetful species — Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus among them — culminating, the tradition holds, in a final and uncorrupted recitation delivered through Muhammad: the Qur'an.
