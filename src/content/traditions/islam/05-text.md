People ask if I understand every word I recite. Not all of it — the Arabic is older and higher than my own. But understanding is not quite the point, or not the only point.

The Qur'an is not a book I read so much as a sound I make. The word for it means *recitation*; it was heard before it was ever written down. So when I take a breath at the start of a verse and let the long vowels open, something happens that has nothing to do with information. My chest does it. The room does it. I have wept at passages whose meaning I already knew cold, purely from the shape of them in the air.

They say the person who memorises the whole of it carries it in the chest, not the head. I understand that now. On the days I cannot pray well, cannot concentrate, cannot feel anything — the sound is still there, waiting, and I climb back in through it.
