It is still dark when the call comes, not from a minaret here but from the phone on the nightstand, the *adhan* rising thin and unhurried into a sleeping apartment. *Prayer is better than sleep.* You believe it and you resent it in equal measure, and you get up anyway.

At the sink you begin *wudu*, the washing that comes before you are allowed to stand before God. Hands, mouth, nose, face; arms to the elbow; a wet hand over the head; the feet. It is not about dirt. It is a small ceremony of readiness, the body announcing to itself that the next few minutes are different from the rest.

You unroll the mat and turn it until it faces Mecca, a direction you could point to blindfolded now, the whole earth quietly oriented around a single distant point. You raise your hands to your ears. *Allahu akbar*, God is greater. Greater than the morning, than the deadline, than the argument you are still half-having in your head.

Then you stand, and bow, and go down until your forehead is on the floor, the highest part of you laid against the lowest place, and for the length of that prostration you are one body among a hundred million others turning toward the same point at the same grey hour, and the smallness of you is, briefly, an enormous relief.
