Belief in Islam is spare and load-bearing. Six articles of faith carry most of the weight: belief in **God**, in his **angels**, in his **revealed books**, in his **messengers**, in a coming **Day of Judgement**, and in **divine decree** — that nothing falls outside God's knowing.

The whole of it can be entered through a single sentence, the **Shahada**: *There is no god but God, and Muhammad is his messenger.* To say it sincerely, before witnesses, is to become Muslim. Notice the shape of it — first a clearing away of every false absolute, then the naming of the one true one. Faith begins as subtraction.

From belief follows action, gathered into the **Five Pillars**: the testimony itself, the five daily prayers, the giving of alms, the fast of Ramadan, and the pilgrimage to Mecca. Islam resists any sharp line between what one believes and what one does at dawn; the creed is meant to be walked, washed, fasted, and bowed, not merely held in the mind.

Underneath sits the conviction that God is at once utterly beyond comprehension and *closer than the jugular vein* — transcendent and intimate in the same breath.
