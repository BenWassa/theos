Christianity begins with a claim that existence is not an accident. Before there was anything, there was God — and creation is spoken, not stumbled into. *In the beginning was the Word,* the Gospel of John opens, echoing the first line of Genesis, *and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* The universe is uttered into being by a mind that intends it, and what is made is called good.

Yet the story does not stay in the garden. Something fractures. The tradition names this rupture *sin* — less a checklist of misdeeds than a broken relationship, a turning-away that runs through every human heart and through creation itself. The cosmos remains good and remains wounded at the same time.

Onto this the tradition lays its most distinctive and scandalous idea: that God does not stay distant from the wreckage but enters it. The Word becomes flesh. The infinite takes a particular body, in a particular place, and dies a particular death — so that the separation might be closed from the inside. Whatever else Christianity is, it is the story of a God who comes looking.
