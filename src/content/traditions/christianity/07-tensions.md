The hard part is not the miracles. I can hold the miracles. The hard part is the child's funeral, and being asked to sing at it.

People outside think faith is the absence of doubt. It isn't, not the kind I've met. It's doubt that keeps showing up anyway. I have prayed into what felt like a closed ceiling and heard nothing come back, and the strange thing is that the tradition already knows about the closed ceiling. It put the words in the mouth of its own God: *My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* If he could say that from the cross, then my worst night is not a disqualification. It's practically liturgical.

I don't have the problem of suffering solved. I have never met anyone honest who does. What I have is a refusal to leave, and a suspicion, some mornings stronger than others, that the one I am shouting at is still, somehow, in the room.
