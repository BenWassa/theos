Hinduism is less a single creed than a family of paths, but a handful of ideas recur across almost all of them.

**Atman and Brahman.** At the core of the person is an undying self, and behind the whole cosmos is an ultimate reality; the great traditions differ on whether these are identical, related, or eternally distinct, and that disagreement is itself central, not a flaw to be resolved.

**Karma.** Action carries consequence, morally and across lifetimes. Not fate, but a law as reliable as gravity: what one does shapes what one becomes.

**Samsara.** The self is not born only once. It travels through a long chain of births and deaths, driven onward by karma and by desire.

**Moksha.** The aim of the highest path is release, liberation from the wheel entirely, an awakening from the dream of separateness back into what was always the case.

**Dharma.** Threaded through all of it is duty, the right way of living appropriate to one's nature and place, the order that holds both a person and a cosmos together.

Crucially, the tradition holds these without demanding that everyone reach them the same way. *As people approach me, so I receive them,* says Krishna in the Gita; *all paths lead to me.* Plurality is not tolerated here so much as built into the foundation.
