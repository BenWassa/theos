You have bathed before the sun is properly up, because you do not come to the shrine carrying the night. The house is quiet. In the small alcove the images wait, brass and colour, a garland gone slightly soft from yesterday.

You strike a match and light the lamp, the *diya*, and the little flame changes the whole corner. Sandalwood catches; the smoke climbs. You ring the small bell, a bright, deliberate sound, not to summon a statue but to wake a guest, to say *I am here, and so, I trust, are you.* Because that is what you are doing: not staring at an object but **hosting**. The divine has consented to take a form you can bring flowers to, and you bring them, marigolds, water, a little fruit, offered with both hands.

You circle the lamp before the images, the flame drawing slow rings in the dark, and for a moment the enormous impersonal cosmos of your scriptures, the wheels of time, the formless absolute, narrows to something you can love at close range: a face, a flame, a smell of sandalwood, a guest in your house at dawn. Then you take a little of what you offered and eat it, *prasad*, grace made edible, and go to start the day already fed.
