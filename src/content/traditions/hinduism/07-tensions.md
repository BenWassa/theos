Do not let anyone sell you the serene version. The tradition is beautiful and it is also the ground on which caste grew, and I will not pretend those are unrelated. The same texts that lift me have been used to tell people where they were allowed to stand. That some of the fiercest attacks on caste have come from *inside* Hinduism — saints, reformers, whole devotional movements insisting God has no interest in your birth — is the tradition arguing with itself, and I would rather stand with that argument than with a tidy defence.

My quieter tension is simpler. I was raised to bow to many gods and taught, in the same breath, that there is finally only One. Outsiders call this a contradiction. I have stopped experiencing it as one. The many forms are how a formless thing lets itself be loved — the way a single white light will still break into colours if you give it something to pass through. But I will admit there are mornings at the shrine when I genuinely do not know whether I am talking to a person or to everything, and I have decided that not-knowing is allowed to be part of the devotion rather than a failure of it.
