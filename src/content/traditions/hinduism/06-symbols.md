If Islam turns toward the abstract and Christianity toward light, Hinduism turns toward **abundance**, a deliberate, joyful surplus of the senses, on the conviction that the divine can be met through the body's delight rather than only its restraint.

At the still centre is a single sound: **Om**, held to be the primordial vibration from which the cosmos unfolds, the note the universe is humming under everything. Around it blooms everything loud. **Colour** is theological here, the marigold's orange, vermilion on the forehead, the drenching pigments of Holi. **Fire** is constant: the small *diya* flame circled before an image, the great lamps of the evening *arti*, the final fire of the cremation ground. **Sound** layers bell and conch and chant. **Scent** means sandalwood and jasmine and camphor smoke.

And at the heart of the home and temple stands the **murti**, the image of the deity, understood by practitioners not as a symbol standing in for an absent god but as a form the divine has graciously entered so that it can be seen, dressed, fed, and loved. The whole sensory apparatus serves a single move: to make the immeasurable small enough to hold, and warm enough to adore.
