I came to the Gita expecting comfort and instead got a battlefield. That is where it is set — a man frozen between two armies, unable to act, and God as his charioteer telling him, essentially, to get up.

The line I carry is the one about the fruits. *You have a right to your work, but never to its fruits.* I misread it for years as permission not to care. It is the opposite. It says: pour everything into the doing, and let go of the outcome — because the outcome was never the part that belonged to you. I run a small business. I have wanted to have that verse tattooed somewhere I could see it at 3 a.m.

What moves me is that it does not ask me to leave my life to find God. It finds Arjuna at the worst moment of his, with a job he doesn't want to do, and says the path runs straight through the doing of it. My kitchen, my accounts, my duty — the text keeps insisting these are not the obstacle. They are the road.
