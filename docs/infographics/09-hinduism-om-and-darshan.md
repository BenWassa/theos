## Purpose (one sentence)

Pair the two intimacies at the heart of Hindu worship: **Om**, the primordial
sound the universe is humming under everything, and **darshan**, the mutual gaze
between worshipper and deity.

## The idea to communicate

From the prose: *"At the still centre is a single sound: Om, held to be the
primordial vibration from which the cosmos unfolds, the note the universe is
humming under everything."* And on darshan: *"the act of seeing and being seen by
the deity, so that a temple visit is less about hearing a sermon than about a
mutual gaze."*

A reader should leave with:
1. **Om (Aum)** — the primordial sound/vibration; the still centre.
2. **Darshan** — worship as *seeing and being seen* — a two-way gaze, not a
   lecture.
3. Together they frame Hindu devotion as **sound + sight**, intimate and mutual —
   the closing move of "making the immeasurable small enough to hold."

## The photo (the "window")

- **Subject:** the **eyes of a murti** or the meeting of gaze at a shrine — a
  deity's painted eyes, a lamp-lit shrine face, hands presenting a lamp to an
  image (arti). Darshan is *about the eyes*, so an image where the gaze is present
  is ideal. (Depicting a deity image is appropriate for Hinduism — unlike the
  Islam pieces — because the murti and darshan are the literal subject.)
- **Placement:** a centred window — the gaze looks out at the reader, enacting
  darshan. Portrait format supports a strong central axis.
- **Treatment:** orange duotone, shadows to `bg #1a0803`, lamp-glow highlights to
  `#fb923c`/`#fdc294`. Grain ~4%. Preserve the eyes' clarity — don't crush them.
- Sourcing: "Hinduism · Om & Darshan" lens set in
  [`photo-picker.html`](photo-picker.html).

## Composition — sound above, gaze below

```
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│  SENSORY WORLD · HINDUISM     │  ← kicker orange

│                               │

│      the primordial sound     │     "the note under everything"; a single
│      ·  ·  ·  ·  ·             │     ripple/soundline under it (calm arcs)

│      │   T H E        │        │
│      │   G A Z E      │◄──►    │  ← two small opposed arrows = mutual seeing
│      │  (murti eyes)  │        │
│      └───────────────┘        │
```

Two stacked halves: **Om above** (sound — the om glyph + a single calm soundline)
and **darshan below** (sight — the photo + a two-headed arrow marking the *mutual*
gaze). The vertical axis reads sound-into-sight, the two intimacies of worship.

## Type

- Kicker: `SENSORY WORLD · HINDUISM`, sans orange, `+0.22em`.
- Title: `Om & Darshan`, serif 500, `#fff7ed`.
- `OM` / `DARSHAN` labels: sans 500, 15px, `+0.16em`, `#fb923c`.
- Definitions: serif 400, 16px, `#c9a48a`; the "mutual gaze" line may be serif
  italic in `#fff7ed`.

## Iconography

The **om** glyph is a *featured element* here, not just a watermark — render it
large and solid in the orange accent as the "sound" half. This is the one piece
where the symbol is foreground. Keep the same om path used across the site.

## Piece-specific don'ts

- Don't reduce darshan to a one-way "look at the god" — the whole point is
  *mutual*; the two-headed arrow (or two facing eyes) must be present.
- Don't use a jagged audio waveform for Om — a single calm ripple/soundline, in
  keeping with the reverent register.
- One ember: warm oranges only; the om and soundline share the accent.

## Accessibility & alt text

The om glyph must have a text label ("Om") — never rely on the symbol alone.
Text over the photo's gaze region ≥4.5:1. Ship:

> Alt: "The syllable Om rendered large in orange above a calm soundline, over a
> lamp-lit photograph of a deity's eyes meeting the viewer's gaze. Labels explain
> Om as the primordial sound at the still centre and darshan as worship that is
> seeing and being seen by the deity — a mutual gaze, shown with a two-headed
> arrow."
